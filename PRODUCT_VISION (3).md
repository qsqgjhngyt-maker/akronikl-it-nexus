# Third-Party Materials and Provenance Policy

Nexus cannot grant more rights than it owns. Every dependency and external source therefore needs a record of origin, version, rights holder, license, required notices, affected files, modification/distribution rights, commercial restrictions, and status (`approved`, `replace`, `review`, `unknown`).

The current C++ baseline loads JSCPP from a CDN. JSCPP's public repository identifies it as MIT-licensed. Migration should preserve the required notice and pin a specific version/hash rather than rely on an unpinned latest asset.

For textbooks/PDFs, facts and ideas can generally be independently explained, but protected expression should not be copied or closely rewritten without a legal basis. Each source PDF needs its own provenance record.

AI-generated drafts still require human editorial review, factual checking and provenance review. AI must not be used to disguise copying from a specific source.

Stable public releases must not contain `unknown` or `replace` rights status.
