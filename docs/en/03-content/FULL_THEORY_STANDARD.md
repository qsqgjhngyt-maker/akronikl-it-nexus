# Full Theory Standard

## Purpose

The full-theory block must be readable as a self-contained textbook section. It must not assume that a beginner already knows terms such as IDE, CLI, API, runtime, namespace, linker, or similar professional vocabulary; terms and abbreviations are expanded when they first become relevant.

## Required qualities

- precise definitions;
- expansion of terms, abbreviations, and notation at first meaningful use;
- motivation and placement in the knowledge system;
- causal explanation rather than isolated facts;
- an internal execution/data model;
- several genuinely different examples;
- edge cases and limitations;
- common errors and diagnostics;
- modern practice;
- historical context only when useful and clearly separated;
- conclusion and self-check.

## Length

There is no artificial character target. A simple topic may be 3–5k characters; a complex one may be 8–15k+ when justified. Completeness and clarity matter more than length.

## Prohibited

Template text with only topic names swapped, repeated generic analogies, unsupported claims, unexplained jargon, and mixing obsolete practice into the modern baseline without clear labeling.
